# A problem you owned end to end

"Tell me about a time you owned a problem end to end."

Answer out loud, as you would in the room, and aim for about two minutes. Then score yourself against the rubric.

Use a real story from your own work. A made-up or borrowed story falls apart at the first follow-up question, and your real experience is what the interviewer wants to hear.

A strong answer shows:

- **Why it was yours.** Maybe it wasn't your team's job, or nobody else had picked it up. Say so in a sentence.
- **The whole arc.** Noticing the problem, finding the cause, fixing it, cleaning up the damage, and making sure it stays fixed.
- **Your decisions.** What you chose to do, why, and what you decided against.
- **A measurable result.** Time saved, errors removed, money recovered, or customers no longer affected.
- **What happened after.** A test, an alert, a runbook, or a clear owner, so the fix outlasts your attention.
- **A reflection.** What you learned or would do differently.

If you're short on stories, check your story bank. The same project often works for ownership and for a customer or failure question.
