# A hard problem fixed with a customer

"Tell me about a time you worked directly with a customer to fix a hard problem."

Answer out loud, as you would in the room, and aim for about two minutes. Then score yourself against the rubric.

Use a real story from your own work. A made-up or borrowed story falls apart at the first follow-up question, and your real experience is what the interviewer wants to hear.

A strong answer shows:

- **Direct contact.** You were on the call, in the thread, or on site with the customer, not just working a ticket someone else wrote up.
- **The goal behind the problem.** What the customer was trying to get done, and what the failure was costing them.
- **How you found the cause.** The questions you asked, the data you looked at, and how you narrowed it down.
- **Your decisions.** What you built or changed, why, and what you decided against.
- **A result for the customer.** Fewer failures, less time lost, or a relationship saved, with numbers where you have them.
- **A reflection.** What you'd do earlier next time, or what you changed so other customers won't hit the same problem.

If you're short on stories, check your story bank. The same project often works for customer work and for an ownership or ambiguity question.
